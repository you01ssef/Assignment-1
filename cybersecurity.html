Course Connection

My Cybersecurity Awareness

Connecting course concepts to real-world IT: definitions, asset protection, active 2FA habits, and incident alert triaging.

QUESTION 1

What does cybersecurity mean to you?

To me, cybersecurity means the protection of individuals along with important and sensitive information. It ensures that everyday users and organizations can operate securely without fearing that private data, personal identities, or critical communications will be intercepted, altered, or stolen by unauthorized parties.

Key Definition: Safeguarding people, digital identities, and sensitive records against unauthorized access.
QUESTION 2

Why is cybersecurity important?

Cybersecurity is important to protect assets, information, business secrets, and confidential paperwork. In today's digital environment, intellectual property, corporate finances, and sensitive documents are stored on networks. Without strong security measures, organizations risk severe financial loss, stolen trade secrets, and compromised confidential records.

Why It Matters: Preserving business continuity, protecting trade secrets, and preventing confidential data breaches.
QUESTION 3

What is one cybersecurity habit you currently practice?

One cybersecurity habit I actively practice is using Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) across all my important personal, academic, and financial accounts. A second factor makes a stolen password less useful to an attacker. It reduces risk, although phishing and stolen sessions can still bypass some forms of 2FA.

QUESTION 4

What cybersecurity topic do you want to learn this semester?

While my primary career focus is general IT infrastructure rather than cybersecurity specialization, the specific topic I would like to learn about this semester is security alert and incident triaging. In IT support, administrators receive numerous alerts and notifications daily. Learning how to triage—quickly evaluating an alert, determining its severity, filtering noise, and knowing how to prioritize urgent threats—is an essential practical skill for any IT administrator.

Learning Goal: Incident triaging, alert prioritization, and efficient threat escalation workflows.
